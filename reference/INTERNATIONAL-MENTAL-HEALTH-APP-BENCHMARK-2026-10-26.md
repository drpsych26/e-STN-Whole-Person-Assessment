# STN International Benchmark Review — Screening, Triage and Digital Mental Health
**Research checked:** 26 October 2026  
**Purpose:** extract relevant design, evidence, safety, privacy and service-delivery lessons for the STN Whole Person Initial Assessment.  
**Status:** desk research, not an audit of vendors, not a clinical validation of STN, and not a substitute for professional/legal review.

## Executive conclusion
STN should not copy a single consumer app. Its intended role—whole-person intake, screening, triage, clinician review, decision support and care planning—is closer to a clinician-facing workflow than to a self-help app. The most useful benchmark combination is:
- APA App Evaluation Model as the evaluation framework;
- Calm Health as an example of validated symptom screening feeding personalised plans and care navigation;
- PTSD Coach as an example of clearly bounded self-management, symptom tracking and support links that explicitly does not replace professional care;
- MindShift CBT as an example of evidence-informed self-management with transparent limits and user-controlled export;
- Wysa as a reminder that AI support needs explicit scope limits and crisis disclaimers;
- MindDoc as an example of longitudinal symptom tracking and self-management, while checking current product claims, privacy and regulatory details at each release.
One Mind PsyberGuide adds structured credibility, user experience and transparency review; it should be treated as a review framework/database, not proof that an app is safe for every clinical use.

## Important limitation
Public product pages describe vendor claims and intended functions. They do not establish that every implementation is clinically effective, that every safety workflow is reliable, or that STN may lawfully reuse a questionnaire. The current STN code has not been independently assessed against these vendors or clinically validated by this desk research. Do not claim equivalence, endorsement, certification or superiority.

## Benchmark findings

### 1. MindDoc (formerly Moodpath)
**Publicly described role:** symptom and mood tracking, self-management courses/exercises, and monitoring over time. The current Apple App Store listing describes symptom tracking and self-management and identifies the product as an EU MDR Class I medical product; it also discloses privacy practices that may include identifiers and usage data for tracking/analytics/marketing. These are developer disclosures in the listing, not an independent audit.  
**STN lesson:** longitudinal trends should be separate from one-off screening results; show date ranges and changes over time, allow users/clinicians to interpret context, and make privacy/data-sharing disclosures specific. Do not infer diagnoses from tracking alone.  
**Source:** https://apps.apple.com/au/app/minddoc-mental-health-support/id1052216403

### 2. Calm Health
**Publicly described role:** invitation-only employer/health-plan/clinical-team access; short screening based on PHQ-9 and GAD-7; screening and self-reported goals inform a personalised plan with clinical programs and sponsored support resources. The vendor says its programmes draw on CBT, ACT and DBT, and says availability varies by region/sponsor.  
**STN lesson:** screening should lead to a transparent next-step plan, not stop at a score. Distinguish screening from diagnosis; make the recommendation rationale visible; support referral to services actually available to the person; avoid promising access where local service pathways are not confirmed.  
**Source:** https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/27054399791643-Calm-Health-FAQs and https://health.calm.com/employers/

### 3. Wysa
**Publicly described role:** AI-enabled wellbeing coach, digital tools and, in some offerings, human coaching/care-team support. Its public website explicitly says it is not designed for crisis or emergencies and does not provide medical/clinical advice; it directs users to professional or country-specific crisis support.  
**STN lesson:** if AI is added, define allowed use, prohibit autonomous diagnosis/crisis decisions, disclose AI use and limits, monitor bias/failure, provide safe handoff, and never imply a chatbot is emergency response. A disclaimer alone is not an adequate clinical safety workflow.  
**Sources:** https://www.wysa.com/ and https://legal.wysa.io/privacy-policy

### 4. PTSD Coach
**Publicly described role:** a US VA/Department of Defense-origin app offering PTSD information, symptom tracking/screening, self-management tools and support links. The VA says the PCL is a reliable/valid self-report measure but does not replace professional evaluation; the app itself is not a substitute for professional treatment. The VA also notes that self-monitoring data are only as secure as the user’s device unless shared with a provider.  
**STN lesson:** clearly label instrument version and purpose; preserve distinction between screen and professional evaluation; provide support links and self-management only within approved boundaries; explain device/data-sharing limitations. Localise support resources for New Zealand and test that links are current.  
**Sources:** https://mobile.va.gov/app/ptsd-coach and https://www.ptsd.va.gov/appvid/mobile/ptsdcoach_app.asp

### 5. MindShift CBT
**Publicly described role:** CBT-based anxiety self-management, check-ins, goal tracking, progress monitoring and exportable user data. The provider states it is not designed for severe conditions or crisis intervention. A peer-reviewed 16-week open-label trial of 380 adults in Canada/US reported improvements in anxiety, depressive symptoms, functioning and quality of life; because it was open-label and lacked a randomized control group, these findings should not be treated as proof of causal effectiveness or generalised to STN.  
**STN lesson:** build self-management as a clearly optional adjunct, with explicit suitability boundaries, user-controlled data sharing, and outcome monitoring. Evidence should be described with study design and limitations, not as an unqualified “proven” claim.  
**Sources:** https://www.mindshiftcbt.com/ ; https://mindshiftcbt.com/for-clinics ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244177/

## Evaluation frameworks and what STN should adopt

### APA App Evaluation Model
The American Psychiatric Association’s model is a structured decision aid rather than a universal pass/fail certification. Its current material covers:
1. Accessibility/background and business model;
2. Privacy and security, including data handling, third parties, safety response, breach response and AI;
3. Clinical foundation and supporting evidence;
4. Engagement style/usability;
5. Data integration toward therapeutic goals, including whether information can be shared usefully with care teams.
The APA states its model was updated in April 2025 to include health equity, social factors and AI considerations. Use it as a review checklist for each STN release and each third-party integration—not as an app-store score or endorsement.  
**Sources:** https://www.psychiatry.org/psychiatrists/practice/mental-health-apps/the-app-evaluation-model and https://www.psychiatry.org/psychiatrists/practice/mental-health-apps

### One Mind PsyberGuide
PsyberGuide’s published approach considers credibility, user experience and transparency, including intervention specificity, research evidence, development processes and privacy-policy transparency. Its research paper reported variability in reliability across some items and found credibility ratings were not strongly correlated with app-store stars. It is therefore a useful supplementary review lens, not a substitute for direct technical audit, source validation or clinical governance. Confirm current availability and recency of any individual app review before relying on a rating.  
**Sources:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8558599/ and https://www.ispor.org/publications/journals/value-outcomes-spotlight/vos-archives/issue/view/wearables-making-a-mark-in-digital-health/navigating-the-digital-mental-health-landscape-one-mind-psyberguide

### MIND / APA App Advisor
The APA describes an evaluation process that prompts review of access/background, privacy/security, clinical foundation, usability and therapeutic data integration; it deliberately avoids treating a static score as universally correct because apps change and suitability depends on the clinical context. The MIND database was described by APA as collecting objective information about developer, cost, features, privacy, evidence and data sharing. Verify current database status and records before using it for procurement.  
**Sources:** https://www.psychiatry.org/psychiatrists/practice/mental-health-apps and https://www.psychiatry.org/news-room/apa-blogs/americans-top-5-new-years-resolutions

## STN feature recommendations derived from the comparison

### Clinical workflow
- Keep STN as a clinician-governed whole-person assessment and care-planning workflow; do not turn it into a generic chatbot or consumer mood tracker.
- Show screening result, instrument/version, completeness, limitations, and source for any scoring rule.
- Distinguish screening, triage, clinical formulation, diagnostic decision support and clinician-made diagnosis.
- Give each recommendation a traceable reason and clear next step, including referral or clinician review when indicated.
- Add longitudinal trend views only after reliable time-stamped records and appropriate interpretation controls exist.
- Keep self-management recommendations optional and distinct from clinical decisions.

### Safety and crisis response
- Build an explicit human-owned safety workflow: urgency level, responsible person/role, acknowledgement, handover, documentation, follow-up and timeout/unavailable-clinician pathway.
- Positive, uncertain, declined or missing safety answers must not silently become “no risk”.
- Do not use AI or an overall score as an autonomous crisis decision-maker.
- Localise emergency and crisis resources for New Zealand; confirm availability and ownership of each link before release.
- Test offline, low-bandwidth, interrupted-session and shared-device cases. A disclaimer does not replace an operational escalation pathway.

### Privacy, data and AI
- Maintain a data inventory: every field, purpose, access role, storage location, retention period, sharing recipient, vendor, and deletion path.
- Disclose any AI use, data sent to AI providers, purpose, retention, limitations, bias controls, human oversight and opt-out where applicable.
- Provide export/access/correction and retention/disposal workflows.
- Audit third-party SDKs, analytics and advertising; default to no advertising trackers in a clinical assessment.
- Do not assume a vendor’s HIPAA/GDPR/ISO claim proves STN compliance or that those regimes substitute for New Zealand requirements.

### Usability, equity and access
- Support tablet, mobile and desktop; clear language; keyboard/screen-reader use; accessible contrast; save/resume; error recovery; and appropriate timeouts.
- Design for low bandwidth and rural access, but never represent offline data as synchronised or escalated until confirmed.
- Test Māori and other cultural/linguistic appropriateness with appropriate partners; include equity and accessibility in acceptance criteria rather than adding them after launch.
- Ensure care pathways reflect actual New Zealand services and eligibility, not international service directories.

### Governance and evidence
- Maintain a versioned instrument/licence register and clinical content change-control log.
- Use a per-release APA-style review and PsyberGuide-style credibility/transparency review, with named reviewers and dated evidence.
- Maintain a clinical safety case, risk register, incident/near-miss log, model card/AI facts label if AI is used, and reproducible golden tests.
- Do not market STN as clinically validated, diagnostic, certified or superior until the specific claim has suitable evidence and required approvals.

## New Zealand-specific note
The Office of the Privacy Commissioner states that the Health Information Privacy Code 2020 applies to identifiable health information handled by health agencies. The current code was amended to incorporate IPP3A from 1 May 2026. STN’s privacy assessment must use the version currently in force and obtain qualified NZ review; international app policies are not a substitute.  
**Source:** https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/codes-of-practice/hipc2020/

## Proposed STN benchmark acceptance checklist
- [ ] Every STN feature has a stated user/patient need and intended purpose.
- [ ] Every screening instrument is licensed/authorised and version-controlled.
- [ ] Every scoring rule has source traceability and golden tests.
- [ ] Every safety route has an accountable human, acknowledgement and failure-path test.
- [ ] No diagnosis or safety status is inferred from generic totals or missing data.
- [ ] AI usage, limitations, data flow and human oversight are disclosed and reviewed.
- [ ] Privacy notice, consent/authority, access/correction, retention, deletion and breach handling are approved.
- [ ] Third-party services, SDKs, data sharing and cross-border processing are reviewed.
- [ ] Accessibility, cultural safety, low-bandwidth and device workflows pass UAT.
- [ ] Evidence claims are matched to study designs and limitations.
- [ ] A named clinical owner and security/privacy reviewers sign off the release evidence.
- [ ] Release remains HOLD until all required gates pass.
