# STN — whole-person mental-health assessment and care coordination

## Purpose

Supportive Therapy Network Limited is developing a digital platform intended to make mental-health assessment and care planning more coordinated, understandable and accessible. The concept brings together presenting concerns, wellbeing domains, safety needs, functional impacts, clinician formulation, agreed goals and referrals into a more coherent pathway.

## Need and proposed approach

People may face repeated storytelling, fragmented assessments, unclear referrals and difficulty coordinating mental-health, addiction, primary-care and wrap-around supports. STN intends to support structured whole-person intake, screening, human-led safety review, clinician-led formulation, transparent diagnostic decision support, person-centred care plans and consent-based coordination.

## Principles

- Person-centred, trauma-informed, culturally safe and accessible.
- Screening, formulation and formal diagnosis remain distinct.
- Qualified humans remain accountable for clinical decisions and safety response.
- Privacy, informed consent, least-privilege access and auditable disclosures.
- Integration only through approved interfaces and written agreements.
- Claims and outcomes are evaluated rather than assumed.

## Current maturity

**Prototype and early development assets only.** Current materials include browser prototypes, a paper-pilot form, a Flutter MVP scaffold and draft technical/governance specifications. Available evidence does not establish clinical validation, production security, real-client readiness, agency integration or endorsement by Health New Zealand, Corrections or any other agency. Demonstration questions and scores must not be called validated instruments without exact-version permissions and evidence.

## Proposed stages

1. Requirements traceability against the approved paper pilot.
2. Engineering completion and automated regression testing.
3. Independent clinical, cultural-safety, accessibility, privacy and security review.
4. Approved supervised feasibility pilot with safety coverage, stop criteria and evaluation.
5. Evaluate usability, completion, referral appropriateness, workload, equity, safety events and client experience.
6. Controlled scale-up only if evidence, approvals, funding and operations support it.

Potential benefits such as reduced duplication, clearer plans and easier referral follow-through are intended outcomes, not proven results. Use synthetic data in demonstrations and do not imply partnerships, procurement approval or integrations without written confirmation.
